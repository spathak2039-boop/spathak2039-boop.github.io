About

I am an Associate Professor of Mathematics at the University of Utah Asia Campus in Incheon, South Korea. I earned my PhD in Applied Mathematics from the University of New Mexico and my MS in Mathematical Sciences from Florida International University. My academic work brings together mathematical analysis, teaching, and research on how students learn mathematics.

A central theme in my teaching is that students should not only learn how to perform mathematical procedures, but also understand what the mathematics means. I am particularly interested in making connections among symbolic, graphical, and conceptual ways of thinking.

Research

Applied Mathematics

My applied-mathematics research focuses on the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations, including maximum-norm estimates, bounded and nondecaying initial data, half-space domains, and analysis of the pressure term.

Navier–Stokes equationsPDEsMaximum-norm estimatesPressure analysisFourier analysis

Mathematics Education

My mathematics-education research examines students’ understanding of functions and graphs, transitions between algebraic and graphical representations, graphical reasoning, and the design of mathematics curricula and textbooks in Nepal.
